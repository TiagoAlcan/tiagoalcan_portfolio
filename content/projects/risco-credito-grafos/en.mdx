<CaseStudy.Context>

A business credit risk model usually looks at each company in isolation: its numbers, its history. But no company fails alone. If its main supplier or its main customer is in trouble, its risk changes, and that does not show up in its own data.

</CaseStudy.Context>

<CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Step title="The proposal">
My proposal was to treat <Mark>each company's supply chain as a weight</Mark> in the risk classification: who depends on whom, and how much.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Why a graph">
Dependency between companies is a relationship, and relationships are what a graph database stores as first-class data. The question "who is affected if this company fails?" becomes a path in the graph instead of a chain of table joins.
</CaseStudy.Step>

</CaseStudy.Process>
