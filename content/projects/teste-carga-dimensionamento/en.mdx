<CaseStudy.Context>

The client had to decide how much to invest in servers for an application. The question was a business one, but the answer lived in technical numbers: how much load the system can take, and on which machine.

</CaseStudy.Context>

<CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Generate the load">
The tests ran in JMeter, simulating use of the application under a growing volume of requests.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Read the result like the person paying for it">
A load-test report talks about response times, throughput and errors. Whoever approves the investment needs something else: <Mark>which server to buy</Mark>. The work was making that translation.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Deliver a recommendation, not a report">
The result became a single, justified configuration instead of a table for the client to interpret alone.
</CaseStudy.Step>

</CaseStudy.Process>
