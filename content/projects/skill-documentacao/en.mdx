<CaseStudy.Context>

Installing a product depended on procedures that lived only in the heads of people who had done it before. Each manual came out with a different structure, depending on the author.

A standard that depends on memory is not a standard. The problem had two parts: deciding what the right format was, and making sure it was followed every time.

</CaseStudy.Context>

<CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Step title="First, a manual someone can run alone">
The starting point was a product's installation manual, written to be followed by someone who had never done that procedure.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="From document to standard">
That manual was adopted as the <Mark>company's official documentation standard</Mark>.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="From standard to skill">
A written standard still has to be read and remembered. I encoded the structure and the writing rules in a Claude skill that generates installation, deployment and change manuals in the approved format.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="The person stays responsible for the content">
The skill takes care of form. The technical content comes from whoever knows the system, and that person reviews the result.
</CaseStudy.Step>

</CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Result>

<Metrics>
  <MetricPanel label="Manual types generated" value="3" hint="installation, deployment and change" />
  <MetricPanel label="Official standard" value="1" hint="the same for every document" />
</Metrics>

The format no longer depends on who is writing. The AI applies the standard the same way every time, and its behavior can be read and corrected in the text of the skill itself.

</CaseStudy.Result>
