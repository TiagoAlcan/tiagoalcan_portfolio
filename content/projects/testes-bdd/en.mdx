<CaseStudy.Context>

Every system has quality criteria. The trouble starts when they only exist in the tester's head: the client cannot check them, and the team cannot repeat them.

</CaseStudy.Context>

<CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Write the behavior, not the test">
BDD describes what the system should do in a language that business and engineering people read the same way: given a context, when something happens, then a result is expected.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<Terminal title="scenario.feature (made-up example)" lines={["Scenario: transaction above the limit", "  Given a card with an available limit of R$ 100.00", "  When a purchase of R$ 150.00 is submitted", "  Then the transaction is declined", "  And the stated reason is insufficient limit"]} />

<CaseStudy.Step title="A document the client can check">
Each scenario is documentation and acceptance criterion at once: if the described behavior does not happen, the test fails and everyone understands why.
</CaseStudy.Step>

</CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Result>

The documentation was delivered to the client and <Mark>adopted as their quality bar</Mark>. Criteria that used to be implicit now have a text both sides sign off on.

</CaseStudy.Result>
