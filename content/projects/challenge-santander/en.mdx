<CaseStudy.Context>

The challenge started from two datasets: one with each company's revenue, balance and sector, another with the transactions between them. The question was what a business-portfolio manager can decide by looking at that.

A table does not answer it. The two datasets had to be joined to say, for each company, how its financial health looks, what the risk is and where there is a credit opportunity.

</CaseStudy.Context>

<CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Rules you can explain">
Financial health, risk level and credit opportunity are classified by rules. Whoever reads the result can tell <Mark>why a company landed in each band</Mark>, which an opaque model does not give you.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="From the portfolio down to one company">
The overview has indicators that follow the filters, a table searchable by company ID and Gold, Silver and Bronze tiers. The charts cover health and risk, the relationships between companies (intensity and dependency), a map of opportunities by sector and a scatter of revenue against balance.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="The value chain as a graph">
Each company is shown linked to its customers and suppliers, colored by risk and marked for dependency. It is the same idea as my undergraduate research: a company's risk also lives in whoever it depends on.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<CaseStudy.Step title="Where AI comes in, and where it does not">
When two or more companies are compared, the dashboard shows a written opinion next to the rules. In this version that opinion is **simulated**: the text imitates what a language model would write, without calling a real one.
</CaseStudy.Step>

<Terminal title="terminal" lines={["$ pip install -r requirements.txt", "$ streamlit run santander.py"]} />

</CaseStudy.Process>

<CaseStudy.Result>

The dashboard is published and the repository is public, with the challenge datasets. The classification rules and the graph work on the data provided; the AI opinion is still simulated.

</CaseStudy.Result>
